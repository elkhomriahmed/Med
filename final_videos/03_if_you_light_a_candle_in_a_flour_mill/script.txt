If you light a candle inside a flour mill, the whole building explodes.
You see, flour mills ban every flame. No candles. No lamps. Not even a match in your pocket.
But the new guy just laughs. "It's flour! It makes pancakes, not bombs!"
That night, the mill is freezing. So he sneaks a candle up to the top floor, where the air hangs thick and white.
Then something strange happens. The air around the flame starts to sparkle. Tiny sparks, popping and dancing, like fireflies made of fire.
Then, whump. A ball of flame rolls across the ceiling. One second, and it's gone. He laughs again.
But that tiny puff just shook every beam in the building. Years of dust pour off the rafters like snow. The whole room turns white. And the candle is still burning.
But how can flour, the stuff in your pancakes, blow up a building?
Well, a pile of flour barely burns. But throw it in the air, and every speck gets wrapped in oxygen. One speck catches, lights the next, and the fire races through the cloud faster than you can blink. That first puff was just the warm-up. It shook down the perfect cloud for the real one.
Boom. The second blast is a hundred times bigger. The roof flies off. The walls fold out like cardboard.
And the new guy? He flies out the window, lands in the river, and floats away like a powdered donut.
Real mills learned this the hard way. In 1878, one in Minneapolis blew up so loud, people heard it ten miles away.
So yes, it's flour. And it makes bombs.
