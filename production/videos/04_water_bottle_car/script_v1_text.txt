If you leave a water bottle in your car, your car can catch fire.
You see, everyone warns you about hot cars in summer. No lighters on the dashboard. No spray cans. Nothing left baking in the sun.
But the new guy just laughs. "It's water! Water puts fires out!"
He tosses the bottle on the seat, slams the door, and walks into the office.
Noon. The parking lot is baking. Not a single person in sight.
Then something strange happens. A tiny white dot appears on the driver's seat. Bright. Burning. Dancing every time the bottle rolls.
Then, a thin line of smoke. Then a black spot. Then a smell like burnt toast drifting across the lot.
Upstairs, his coworker glances out the window. "Hey... is that your car?"
He runs. Down the stairs. Across the lot. Keys flying out of his hand.
Inside the car, the dot has grown into a glowing orange ring.
But how can a bottle of water start a fire?
Well, a round, clear bottle works exactly like a magnifying glass. Sunlight hits the curve, and the water bends every ray toward one single point. All that heat, squeezed into a spot smaller than a coin. Hot enough to scorch leather, melt plastic, and light the foam inside your seat.
And the closed windows? They trap the heat inside, like an oven.
He rips the door open just as the seat bursts into flames.
And this really happens. In 2017, a man in Idaho spotted smoke rising off his car seat. The culprit? His water bottle.
So yes. Water puts fires out. Unless it starts them.
