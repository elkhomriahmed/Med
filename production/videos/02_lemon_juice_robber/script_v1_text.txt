He robbed two banks in broad daylight with no mask, because he'd rubbed lemon juice on his face.
You see, lemon juice is invisible ink. Write with it, and the paper stays blank. So he figured, coat your face in it, and the cameras can't see you either.
He even tested it. He squeezes six lemons onto his face, grabs a Polaroid, snaps a selfie, and the photo comes out blank. His eyes are burning, but the plan works.
His buddy just sniffs the air. "Man, you smell like a fruit salad."
He doesn't care. He walks into a bank, looks straight at the security camera, and smiles.
Then he walks into a second bank. Same lemons. Same smile. This time, he waves.
That night, his face is on the eleven o'clock news. In full color. Police are knocking on his door before the weather report ends.
They show him the tape. He stares, honestly confused, and says, "But I wore the juice."
But the story doesn't end in a cell. A psychology professor reads about him and can't stop wondering: how was he so sure?
You see, the skills you need to do something well are the same skills you need to notice you're doing it badly. So the less you know, the more confident you feel.
They named it the Dunning-Kruger effect.
He never got away with the money. But he did end up in psychology textbooks.
