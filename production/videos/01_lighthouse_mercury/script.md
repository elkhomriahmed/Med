# Video 1: Why Lighthouse Keepers Went Mad (script v3, longer + funnier)

Read at a normal fast storytelling pace; the edit speeds it up ~15-20% and keeps it above 60 s.
Every sentence has its own picture in the shot list (see shotlist.md).

> He floated a two-ton glass lens on a bathtub of liquid metal.
>
> You see, the new lighthouse lens was so heavy the old clockwork could barely turn it. The keeper had to crank it by hand all night, and every time he stopped for a sandwich, a ship nearly hit the rocks.
>
> So the company sends a young engineer. He doesn't even look at the gears. He pours a shiny silver pool under the lens, gives it one push with his finger, and two tons of glass start spinning like a record.
>
> The old keeper laughs. "Son, I've turned that thing by hand for thirty years. You can't float glass on a puddle."
>
> But it works. Every night the beam sweeps perfectly, and every morning the keeper polishes that silver bath with a rag, humming, like it's his new best friend.
>
> Then the humming stops.
>
> First he forgets the name of the supply boat. Then he forgets the captain. Then he forgets which one is the boat. His hands shake so hard he spills the lamp oil, twice. By winter he's yelling at the seagulls, convinced they're spying on him.
>
> The whole town laughs. They say thirty years alone on a rock finally cracked him.
>
> But it wasn't the loneliness. That silver bath was mercury. It lets heavy things spin with almost no friction, but it slowly turns into an invisible vapour. And in that tiny sealed lamp room, he was breathing it every single night.
>
> The ships stayed safe. The man keeping them safe didn't.
