# Script 1: "Why Lighthouse Keepers Went Mad" (kept for the first full video)

> He floated a two-ton glass lens on a bathtub of liquid metal.
> You see, the new lighthouse lens was so heavy the old clockwork could barely turn it, and every second it stopped spinning, a ship could miss the warning.
> So the engineer pours a silver pool under it, gives it one push with his finger, and two tons of glass start spinning like a record.
> The old keeper laughs. "Son, I've turned that lens by hand for thirty years. You can't float glass on metal."
> But it works. Every night the beam sweeps perfectly, and every morning the keeper wipes down the silver bath with a rag, humming.
> Then the humming stops.
> First he forgets the name of the supply boat. Then his hands shake so hard he spills the lamp oil. By winter, he's yelling at the seagulls, convinced they're spying on him.
> The town says the loneliness finally got him. The engineer says he was always a little odd.
> You see, that silver bath was mercury. It lets heavy things spin with almost no friction, but it slowly turns into an invisible vapour. And in that tiny sealed lamp room, the keeper was breathing it every single night.
> The ships stayed safe. The man keeping them safe didn't.
